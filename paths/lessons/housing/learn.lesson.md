# A home that fits your life

A smaller monthly payment doesn't always mean a cheaper home. A good comparison
looks at **upfront cash, ongoing costs, and how long you expect to stay**.

<bubu-coach>Let's look at the whole picture. There isn't one right home for everyone.</bubu-coach>

## Meet Sam

Sam may move to another city in two years. Renting leaves more cash available;
buying could build equity, but also brings closing costs, repairs, and selling costs.
These are fictional teaching scenarios, not quotes for a real property.

| Look at | Renting | Buying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront cash | Deposit and moving costs | Down payment, closing and moving costs |
| Ongoing costs | Rent, utilities, insurance | Loan payment, taxes, insurance, maintenance |
| Flexibility | Lease terms and moving costs | Time and cost to sell |

A down payment becomes part of the owner's equity; it isn't simply a lost fee.
But that cash isn't readily available for an unexpected expense either.

## Your move

Say your answer aloud or jot it down: **What two costs would you ask Sam to check
before comparing monthly payments? Which one requires cash upfront?**
